💡 Key Tips for Task 1

  • Never give personal opinions — only report the facts
  • Paraphrase the introduction paragraph using different words
  • Include an Overview paragraph (the most important trends)
  • Reference specific data with numbers (at least 2 numerical references)
  • Use descriptive linking words and varied sentence structures
  • Structure: Introduction → Overview → Body 1 → Body 2
  • Spend about 3 minutes planning, 15 minutes writing, 2 minutes checking

📝 Ready-Made Template for Task 1

Paragraph 1 — Introduction: The chart/graph/table illustrates/shows/presents [what the data is about] in/on [place] between [time period].
Paragraph 2 — Overview: Overall, it is clear that… / The most striking trend is that…
Paragraph 3 — Details 1: According to the data, [Subject] had the highest/lowest… rising from X to Y over the period.
Paragraph 4 — Details 2: In contrast, [Subject] showed a different pattern, with a notable [increase/decrease] from X to Y.

In contrast, renewable energy showed a remarkable upward trajectory. Starting from a mere 5% in 2000, it climbed steadily, surpassing coal by 2015 and reaching approximately 35% by 2020. Solar and wind power together accounted for the bulk of this growth, rising from 2% to 25% over the two decades.

💡 Key Tips for Task 2

  • Read the question carefully — the question type determines your response
  • Your answer must directly address every part of the question (Task Response)
  • Write at least 4 paragraphs: Introduction, 2 Body paragraphs, Conclusion
  • Use specific, real-world examples to support your ideas
  • Each body paragraph should begin with a clear Topic Sentence
  • Use a variety of vocabulary and grammatical structures
  • Don't spend more than 5 minutes planning, and always leave 2–3 minutes to proofread

📝 Ready-Made Template for Task 2

Introduction: It is often argued that… / The question of whether… has sparked considerable debate. In my opinion, I believe that…
Body Paragraph 1: Topic sentence + explanation + example + mini-conclusion
Body Paragraph 2: Topic sentence + explanation + example + mini-conclusion
Conclusion: In conclusion, although…, I firmly believe that…

Sample Essay — Social Media (Opinion: Agree/Disagree)

Some people believe that social media has a negative impact on society. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Click to expand full answer

Introduction: In recent years, social media platforms have become an integral part of daily life for billions of people worldwide. While some argue that these platforms have a detrimental effect on society, I largely disagree with this view, as I believe the benefits of social media far outweigh its drawbacks.

Body 1: One of the most compelling arguments in favour of social media is its ability to connect people across geographical boundaries. Platforms such as Instagram and Twitter enable individuals to maintain relationships regardless of distance. Furthermore, social media has proven to be a powerful tool for raising awareness about social issues. Movements like #MeToo gained momentum largely through viral posts, demonstrating their potential for positive societal change.

Body 2: Admittedly, critics raise valid concerns regarding mental health, particularly among young people. Studies suggest a correlation between excessive screen time and increased rates of anxiety. However, I would argue that the issue lies not with the technology itself, but rather with how individuals choose to use it. With proper digital literacy education, these negative effects can be significantly mitigated.

Conclusion: In conclusion, while social media is not without its challenges, I firmly believe that it serves as a net positive for society. The key lies in promoting responsible usage rather than demonising the platforms themselves.

Sample Essay — Online Education (Discussion + Opinion)

Some people think that online learning is better than classroom learning. Discuss both views and give your opinion.

Click to expand full answer

Introduction: The rise of digital technology has transformed the education sector, with online learning becoming increasingly popular. While some believe that virtual classrooms offer superior advantages, others maintain that traditional face-to-face learning remains more effective.

Body 1 — Online learning advantages: Proponents of online learning argue that it offers unparalleled flexibility. Students can access lectures at any time, which is particularly beneficial for those with work commitments or family responsibilities. Moreover, online courses often cost significantly less than traditional programmes, making quality education accessible to a wider demographic. For instance, platforms like Coursera allow learners in developing countries to access courses from prestigious universities at a fraction of the cost.

Body 2 — Classroom learning advantages: On the other hand, advocates of classroom learning emphasise the importance of direct interaction. The ability to ask questions in real-time and engage in group discussions fosters deeper understanding of complex topics. Additionally, the structured environment of a physical classroom helps many students maintain discipline and focus — qualities that are often lacking in self-directed online study.

Conclusion: In my opinion, the most effective approach combines both methods. Blended learning can harness the flexibility of digital platforms while preserving the social benefits of in-person education, ultimately creating a more well-rounded learning experience.

Sample Essay — Urban Pollution (Problem/Solution)

Air pollution in cities is becoming a serious problem. What are the causes of this issue, and what solutions can you suggest?

Click to expand full answer

Introduction: Urban air pollution has escalated into one of the most pressing environmental challenges of the 21st century. This essay will examine the primary causes of deteriorating air quality in cities and propose several viable solutions.

Body 1 — Causes: The chief cause of urban air pollution is the emission of exhaust fumes from vehicles. With car ownership rates rising exponentially in both developed and developing nations, the volume of pollutants released into the atmosphere has increased dramatically. In addition, industrial activities contribute significantly to the problem, as factories often burn fossil fuels and release toxic gases. A less obvious but equally important factor is the burning of waste in rapidly urbanising areas, which further degrades air quality.

Body 2 — Solutions: To address these issues, governments should invest heavily in public transportation infrastructure. Efficient bus and metro systems would reduce the number of private vehicles on the road. Furthermore, stricter regulations on industrial emissions, enforced through regular inspections and substantial fines, would compel factories to adopt cleaner technologies. Finally, promoting cycling and walking through dedicated lanes and pedestrianised zones could substantially reduce vehicle dependency in city centres.

Conclusion: In summary, urban air pollution is primarily driven by vehicle emissions and industrial activity. However, with the implementation of improved public transport, stricter industrial regulations, and infrastructure encouraging active travel, cities can significantly improve their air quality.

Sample Essay — Remote Work (Advantages/Disadvantages)

Working from home has become increasingly common. Do the advantages of this development outweigh the disadvantages?

Click to expand full answer

Introduction: The shift towards remote work has accelerated dramatically in recent years, fundamentally changing the way millions of people approach their professional lives. While this trend brings certain drawbacks, I believe the advantages substantially outweigh them.

Body 1 — Advantages: The most significant benefit of working from home is the flexibility it provides. Employees can structure their day around personal commitments, leading to improved work-life balance. This flexibility also eliminates commuting time, saving workers an average of one hour per day. For companies, the reduced need for office space translates into considerable cost savings. Research conducted by Stanford University found that remote workers were 13% more productive than their office-based counterparts.

Body 2 — Disadvantages: Nevertheless, remote work is not without its challenges. Many employees report feelings of isolation and disconnection from their colleagues, which can negatively affect both mental health and team collaboration. Moreover, the boundaries between personal and professional life become blurred when the home serves as the workplace, potentially leading to longer working hours and burnout. Additionally, not all roles are suited to remote arrangements, creating equity concerns within organisations.

Conclusion: Although remote work presents legitimate challenges related to social isolation and work-life boundaries, the benefits of enhanced productivity, flexibility, and cost efficiency make it a predominantly positive development for both employees and employers.

Sample Essay — Urbanisation (Two-Part Question)

More people are moving to cities. Why is this happening? Is this a positive or negative development?

Click to expand full answer

Introduction: Over the past few decades, there has been a significant trend towards urbanisation, with an increasing number of people relocating from rural areas to cities. This essay will discuss the key reasons behind this migration and evaluate whether it represents a positive or negative development.

Body 1 — Why people move to cities: The primary driver of rural-to-urban migration is the pursuit of better employment opportunities. The key is for governments to implement effective urban planning to ensure that the benefits of city life are distributed equitably.

Conclusion: In conclusion, the movement towards cities is largely driven by economic and social factors. While the trend has considerable benefits in terms of opportunity and development, it must be managed carefully through thoughtful policy to mitigate the negative consequences of rapid urbanisation.

Sample Essay — Childhood Obesity (Problem/Solution)

In many countries, the number of overweight children is increasing. What problems does this cause, and what can be done to address them?

Click to expand full answer

Introduction: Childhood obesity has become an alarming trend in numerous countries around the world, posing significant health risks to the younger generation. This essay will explore the problems associated with this phenomenon and suggest practical measures to combat it.

Body 1 — Problems: The health implications of childhood obesity are profound and far-reaching. Overweight children face an elevated risk of developing chronic conditions such as type 2 diabetes, cardiovascular disease, and joint problems at a young age. Beyond the physical consequences, obese children often experience bullying and social exclusion, which can lead to depression and low self-esteem. From a societal perspective, the long-term healthcare costs associated with obesity-related illnesses place an enormous burden on public health systems.

Body 2 — Solutions: Several effective strategies can help reverse this trend. First, schools should implement mandatory physical education programmes and ensure that canteen meals meet nutritional standards. Governments could introduce taxes on sugary beverages and junk food, while subsidising fresh fruit and vegetables to make healthier options more affordable. Additionally, parents should be educated about the importance of balanced diets and regular physical activity for their children. Public awareness campaigns, particularly those targeting social media, could also play a significant role in shaping healthier eating habits among young people.

Conclusion: Childhood obesity is a multifaceted problem with serious health and social consequences. However, through a coordinated approach involving governments, schools, and families, it is possible to create environments that encourage healthier lifestyles and reduce the prevalence of obesity among children.

Artificial intelligence is becoming increasingly prevalent in everyday life. Is this a positive or negative development?

Click to expand full answer

Introduction: Artificial intelligence has become an integral part of modern life, from virtual assistants on smartphones to automated systems in healthcare and transportation. While the proliferation of AI raises certain concerns, I believe it is predominantly a positive development.

🎯 General Writing Tips for Band 7+

⏱️ Time Management

  • Task 1: 3 min planning + 15 min writing + 2 min checking
  • Task 2: 5 min planning + 30 min writing + 5 min proofreading
  • Always start with Task 2 — it's worth twice the marks
  • Write your introduction and overview first to guarantee marks

📝 Paragraph Structure

  • Each paragraph = one clear idea
  • Start with a Topic Sentence (the main idea)
  • Follow with an Explanation (why or how)
  • Add an Example (specific evidence)
  • End with a Mini-conclusion linking to the next point

🔒 Task Response Secrets

  • Read the question twice before writing
  • Underline the key words in the question
  • Address every part of the question
  • For opinion essays, state your view clearly in the introduction
  • Never write about topics you can't support with examples

📚 Vocabulary Strategy

  • Use topic-specific vocabulary (not just general words)
  • Avoid repeating the same words — use synonyms
  • Learn collocations (natural word partnerships)
  • Use 2–3 "high-level" words per paragraph (not per sentence)
  • Never use a word you're unsure of — simplicity beats errors

🏗️ Coherence & Cohesion

  • Use paragraphing effectively (one idea per paragraph)
  • Linking words should connect ideas, not be forced in
  • Use referencing: "this," "such," "the former/the latter"
  • Don't overuse connectors — natural flow matters more
  • Topic sentences act as signposts for the reader

✅ Grammar Power Moves

  • Mix simple, compound, and complex sentences
  • Use conditionals: "If governments were to…, it would…"
  • Use passives in Task 1: "It can be seen that…"
  • Use relative clauses: "People who…", "The system which…"
  • Proofread for common errors: subject-verb agreement, articles

🔄 The Ideal 5-Step Writing Process

Step 1: Analyse the Question (2–3 minutes)

  1. Identify the task type — Opinion? Discussion? Problem/Solution?
  2. Underline key words — these tell you exactly what to address
  3. Note any specific instructions — "Give reasons," "Provide examples," etc.
  4. Plan your position — What will you argue? What examples will you use?

Step 2: Plan Your Essay (3–5 minutes)

  1. Decide your thesis — What is your overall position?
  2. List 2–3 main points for each body paragraph
  3. Brainstorm examples for each point
  4. Outline the structure — Introduction → Body 1 → Body 2 → Conclusion

Step 3: Write Your First Draft (25–30 minutes)

  1. Write the introduction — Paraphrase + state your position
  2. Write body paragraphs — Topic sentence → Explanation → Example → Link
  3. Write the conclusion — Summarise + restate your position
  4. Don't stop to edit — keep writing and move forward

Step 4: Edit & Proofread (5 minutes)

  1. Check word count — Must be 150+ (Task 1) or 250+ (Task 2)
  2. Scan for grammar errors — subject-verb, tenses, articles
  3. Check spelling — especially common IELTS words
  4. Read your topic sentences — Do they logically flow?

Step 5: Final Review (1–2 minutes)

  1. Re-read the introduction — Does it match your conclusion?
  2. Check paragraph count — Minimum 4 paragraphs for Task 2
  3. Verify the overview exists for Task 1
  4. Breathe and submit — You've done the work!

🚫 Common Mistakes — Task 1

❌ Adding Personal Opinions

"I think renewable energy is the best option."

✓ "Renewable energy showed the most significant growth."

Task 1 is purely factual. Never include personal views, predictions, or explanations for trends.

❌ Copying the Question Word-for-Word

"The line graph shows the number of tourists visiting…" (copied from question)

✓ "The line graph illustrates changes in tourist numbers across…" (paraphrased)

Always paraphrase the introduction. Use synonyms and restructure the sentence.

❌ Skipping the Overview

Jumping straight into data descriptions after the introduction.

✓ "Overall, the most notable trend was the sharp increase in…"

The Overview paragraph is the most important paragraph in Task 1. It must identify the main trends.

❌ Listing Every Data Point

"In 2000 it was 5%, in 2001 it was 6%, in 2002 it was 7%…"

✓ "The figure rose gradually from 5% in 2000 to approximately 12% by 2005."

Select key data points and group information. Describe trends, don't just list numbers.

❌ Wrong Word Count

Writing only 120 words or exactly 150 words with no room for error.

✓ Aim for 170–190 words for Task 1 (a comfortable margin above 150).

Under-writing loses marks. Over-writing risks mistakes. Aim for 170–190 words.

❌ Poor Time Management

Spending 35 minutes on Task 1 and only 25 minutes on Task 2.

✓ Strictly 20 minutes for Task 1 and 40 minutes for Task 2.

Task 2 is worth twice as much. Always start with Task 2 or set a strict 20-minute timer for Task 1.

🚫 Common Mistakes — Task 2

❌ Not Addressing All Parts of the Question

The question asks "Why?" AND "Is this positive or negative?" but you only answer one.

✓ Address every part of the question with dedicated paragraphs.

Task Response is 25% of your score. Missing part of the question caps you at Band 5–6.

❌ Memorised Introductions

"In today's modern world, it is an undeniably true fact that…" (used for every essay)

✓ "The rapid growth of [topic] has sparked debate about whether…" (tailored)

Examiners recognise templates. Write a specific introduction that directly addresses the question.

❌ Vague or Unsupported Ideas

"Many people think this is bad." (who? where? evidence?)

✓ "A 2023 WHO report found that 35% of urban residents experienced…" (specific)

Support every claim with specific examples, data, or real-world references.

❌ Off-Topic Content

Writing about technology when the question is about education.

✓ Stay on topic and relate every point back to the question.

If you drift off-topic, you lose Task Response marks no matter how well-written your essay is.

❌ Overusing Connectors

"Furthermore, moreover, additionally, in addition, besides, also…" (in one paragraph)

✓ Use 1–2 connectors per paragraph. Let the ideas flow naturally.

Forced linking words hurt Coherence & Cohesion. Quality of connection matters more than quantity.

❌ Informal Language

"Lots of people," "pretty much," "gonna," "stuff," "kinda."

✓ "A significant number of people," "substantially," "approximately," "issues."

IELTS Writing is formal academic English. Avoid contractions, slang, and colloquialisms.

❌ Weak Conclusions

"In conclusion, this essay talked about the topic." (vague and useless)

✓ "In conclusion, while [counterpoint], I believe [thesis] because [reasons]."

Your conclusion should restate your thesis with slightly different wording, not just summarise.

❌ Neglecting to Proofread

Leaving obvious errors: "Their is many reason" / "People should to study"

✓ "There are many reasons" / "People should study" (simple fixes that save marks)

Always leave 3–5 minutes at the end to reread and correct obvious errors.
